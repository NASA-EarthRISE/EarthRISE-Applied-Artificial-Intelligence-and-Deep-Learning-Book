# 12\. Conclusions

The chapters in this book were driven by authors with deep knowledge of a specific problem area and expertise in developing and demonstrating Geospatial Artificial Intelligence to address those challenges. The Editor's goal for this book was to bring together a wide survey of experts with diverse Deep Learning workflows to provide a robust set of examples to support partitioners in growing their skill in the use and application of Deep Learning Techniques, allowing them to adopt these approaches for their challenges. 

A vital takeaway the Editor stresses is that Deep Learning and the field of Artificial Intelligence  are moving quickly, and specific libraries, models, and workflows will age. However, the core skills in these chapters will not: matching a sensor to a phenomenon, shaping data to fit a model, effectively interpreting and analyzing model outputs. Those skills transfer from a U-Net to a foundation model, and they are what let practitioners adapt as new technology arrives.

The future of Artificial Intelligence and GeoAI is likely to be dominated by large language model (LLM) enabled systems and agentic workflows, in which models call tools, retrieve data, and chain analyses together. Therefore, an agentic system will be able to build a pipeline in minutes; however, having critical skills in integrating every aspect of these workflows will likely be vital going forward. Learning these core elements prepares practitioners to direct and evaluate those systems, not simply consume their output.

Further, when developing a workflow for addressing a geospatial challenge, selecting a fit-for-purpose approach is crucial. The newest, biggest, most complicated approach is likely not the place to start. We advise evaluating the whole spectrum, from statistical approaches, classical machine learning, deep learning, and expanding to agentic systems built on foundation models. Across this wide spectrum of complexity and abstraction, seek the best fit-for-purpose approach for your challenge, and balance sophistication with elegance. The book strives to show this and balance the need to demonstrate fit-for-purpose approaches as well as make more novel systems and approaches more transparent. Of course, some of these decisions are also driven by scope, funding and resources.

Additionally, evaluating the skill and uncertainty of the model is critical. Constructing a study design to independently evaluate and test the model's skill and fit-for-purpose utility in a real-world context is essential. In an era of big data and Artificial Intelligence-enabled systems that can rapidly produce outputs, it is essential that practitioners clearly articulate the bounds of their models' capabilities, especially in a real-world context, rather than just reporting incremental metric score increases to standard benchmarks. For these models to have real value, they need to be applied; this is where a practitioner's expertise can further advance the field.

Finally, no model replaces knowing the system you study. Domain knowledge decides which data to collect, which features matter, and whether an output makes sense, and every chapter in this book depends on it. Let your applied knowledge inform every step: the question, the data, the model, and the evaluation. Tools will keep changing. Sound judgment, honest evaluation, and fit-for-purpose design are what make any of them useful.
